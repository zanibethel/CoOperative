# CoOperative Platform Vision

## Product promise

A business owner should be able to say:

> Here is how my business works. Help my team and AI work together better.

CoOperative should then discover the current operation, compare available options, recommend the simplest cost-effective solution, deploy approved workflows, measure what happened, and reuse what proved successful.

## Five product systems

### 1. Intelligence Desk
Builds a structured business model from interviews, connected systems, documents, websites, and observed workflows.

### 2. Capability Registry
A current catalog of what CoOperative can use: native modules, APIs, plugins/connectors, workflow engines, AI models, website components, communication channels, scheduling tools, payment tools, and manual handoffs.

Every capability should record cost model, authentication method, supported actions, limits, risk, and current approval status.

### 3. Playbook Library
A playbook is a reusable solution pattern, not customer data. Example:

`new lead -> qualify -> reply -> schedule -> follow-up -> human exception`

A playbook can have many implementations depending on the customer's tools and cost priorities.

### 4. Mission Control
Executes approved workflows with least-privilege access, approval gates, logs, retries, and human exception handling.

### 5. Improvement Lab
Analyzes evidence across deployments, researches new capabilities, and proposes changes to playbooks or platform code.

Improvement Lab can create a proposal and eventually a pull request. It cannot silently merge or deploy production code.

## Product expansion areas

- inquiry and lead handling
- scheduling and reminders
- support triage
- payment/invoice follow-up
- lightweight websites and landing pages
- forms and customer intake
- social content planning, approval, and supported publishing
- email/SMS follow-up
- CRM-like customer records where a paid CRM is unnecessary
- document processing
- internal knowledge retrieval
- recurring administrative workflows
- analytics and ROI measurement

## Cost philosophy

Do not recommend another subscription simply because one exists.

The planner should compare:

1. existing customer tools,
2. CoOperative native capability,
3. direct API integration,
4. low-cost/open-source workflow infrastructure,
5. paid third-party SaaS.

Choose based on total cost, reliability, maintenance, security, and business value—not sticker price alone.
